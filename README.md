# The Impact of Artificial Intelligence in Astrophysics - BSc Dissertation.
UoM Physics BSc dissertation. The Impact of Artificial Intelligence in Astrophysics.

Supervisor: Prof. Clive Dickinson.

## Abstract
The implementation of artificial intelligence (AI) in astrophysics has increased tenfold
in the past decade in order to deal with the rapidly growing data volumes produced
by modern surveys. This dissertation examines how AI is applied and investigates the
strengths and limitations from both a theoretical stance and an academic community
perspective. A broad overview of major AI applications are outlined, with a focus
on convolutional and Bayesian neural networks (CNNs and BNNs), due to their
current prevalence and potential, respectively. CNNs are examined for galaxy
morphology classification and gravitational wave detection, while BNNs are explored
for cosmological parameter estimation using redshift photometry and exoplanetary
atmospheric retrieval. Across these examples, AI achieves high accuracy and major
increases in efficiency compared to traditional techniques, but can still over-fit to
the training data, mishandle rare examples and provide unreliable uncertainties.
The dissertation concludes that AI will be indispensable for future surveys, but
will require careful implementation, accompanied by transparent communication to
enable understanding and thorough critique.
